# Engineering validation • 23 November 2026

**Use Phase 22 for the screening software and its diagnostic evidence. Hold engineering acceptance of positive-overhang and skewed girder demands.** The zero-overhang correction has an independent load and moment check. For positive overhang, a balanced global load still produces substantial bending in two deck-edge lines for which these fixtures specify no physical steel girders.

## Edge lines and load sharing

In plate_girder/plategirderbridge.py the bridge adds two longitudinal lines for positive deck overhang. It assigns each edge the result of _girder_section() without a girder index. In analyser.py, create_sections and assign_members use that section's A, Iz, Iy, J and shear areas with the same steel material used for physical girders. For these uniform, nonthermal fixtures the sections coincide. The model uses transverse members to connect the lines. The CAD and design inputs specify three or four physical girders. Neither provides an extra steel member at the two edges. The numerical edges can represent a slab boundary, but the supplied inputs do not establish a steel girder EI there. The thermal path can transform main-girder sections and is outside this comparison.

Steel self-weight is prescribed along physical girder lines. OspGrillage._write_op_fix excludes the overhang edge z-groups from the end restraints. Consequently EB1/EB2 carry bending through the connected grid despite having effectively zero direct vertical end reaction. For the straight three-girder case, each edge develops 87.844 kN·m and each physical girder develops 87.137–87.506 kN·m. The independent, simply supported individual-girder reference is 146.452 kN·m. A four-girder bridge gives 97.062–97.325 kN·m per physical girder versus the same reference. The observed fractions of about 0.60 and 0.66 follow the sharing expected among five or six similarly stiff lines. Correct G1…Gn identifiers do not resolve this effect.

The zero-overhang control uses the same span and bare-steel areas, with spacing adjusted to maintain the fixture width. Phase 21 always stripped the outer two lines from the self-weight loop. Phase 22 uses its existing physical-line selection for both geometries. The three-girder zero-overhang result rises from about 48.7 to 145.946 kN·m per girder. The latter is within 0.35% of the continuous-span maximum; the mesh samples on either side of midspan. This comparison establishes the self-weight path for the control. It does not isolate a particular slab-strip width or validate live-load distribution.

A refined grid should reflect actual longitudinal and transverse stiffness. The project needs an agreed construction stage, edge tributary width, deck flexural and torsional stiffness, composite-action assumptions and an independent model of that same section before assigning replacement properties. No alternative EI was selected. See [FHWA Manual for Refined Analysis, §2.4.3](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/bridge/pubs/hif18046.pdf).

## Skew and equilibrium

The warning originates in ospgrillage/utils.py, solve_zeta_eta, during global-to-natural coordinate inversion for load interpolation. It originates before the structural response calculation. In the Phase 21 positive and negative skew fixtures, every stagnation exit had forward position error at most 3.56 × 10⁻¹⁵ m. The branch nevertheless used the returned root without checking status. For opposite horizontal sides, the transverse interpolation fraction follows directly from z, and x varies linearly between the two interpolated sides. Phase 22 inverts these two linear relationships directly, including rotated corner order. Other quadrilaterals use fsolve with status and forward-residual checks. Independent forward-map tests cover corners, center and off-center points.

The skew self-weight deficit persists independently of coordinate convergence. The load vertices in analyser.py span global x=0…25 m at every girder. The oblique mesh shifts each girder longitudinally by roughly z tan(skew). Taking w times the sum of the missed lengths predicts 2.09 and 2.79 kN, consistent with measured deficits. A trial shifting both load vertices exactly onto mesh ends **over-applied** 22.054 and 24.811 kN in these two cases. That trial was rejected. Investigate duplicate contributions where a line coincides with cell boundaries before revising the load distributor. Other skewed line and patch loads have not been independently validated.

## Report integrity

Chapter 4 previously derived both bearing totals from physical-girder element end shears. This omitted nodal loads and transferred forces and reversed one end's sign. Phase 22 sums simultaneous signed nodal Fy at the two support lines, leaves envelope totals unavailable, and withholds totals for cases with significant vertical reaction away from those lines. The custom fixture now reports −76.118 kN at each end, matching direct OpenSees results to displayed precision.

Phase 21 supplied no image payload and disabled figures. The new diagnostic supplies three analysis plots from the application capture functions plus plan, end and isometric views projected from the fixture's OCC solids. The local native viewer attempts terminated with signal 11. The PDF uses an offline CAD renderer and the real report generator with Tectonic. The headless recovery verifies this report path; native viewer capture remains unresolved. See the numerical record for the scope and outcome of the design checks.
